Northwind sales analysis

Code, data instructions and this site: GitHub Repository

Five business questions answered from the Northwind sample database (orders from 1996-07-04 to 1998-05-06), for a manager who wants to understand the business. Each page gives the question and the decision it supports, a short answer, why it matters, the method, the evidence, findings, next steps and limitations.

Main findings

  • Q1. Company sales +120.7% (Jan–Apr): +189,151 from customers buying in both windows, +66,804 from 1998-window-only and -24,987 from 1997-window-only buyers; Canada's and Denmark's declines trace to one 1997-window-only account each.
  • Q2. Top 1/5/10 customers hold 9.9%/34.1%/46.5% of 1997 sales; 33 customers make up 80.2%.
  • Q3. Median order-to-shipment time is 5–8 days in every country with 10+ orders (6–7 days by shipper); orders assigned to United Package and those for the USA and Germany make up most of the slow tail.
  • Q4. Most category growth goes with the quantity effect (+181,916 of +230,968); Dairy Products and Seafood grew broadly; Meat/Poultry's average price jump is mainly associated with product-mix change, with like-for-like price changes also contributing.
  • Q5. Across 56 comparable products, the median of each product's ratio (median discounted quantity ÷ median undiscounted quantity) is 1.19. This summarises products; it does not mean every discounted line bought 19% more. Only Confections' interval excludes 1 — a lead for testing, not proof.

All implications are directions for further evaluation. The analysis is descriptive: it shows associations, not causes.

Questions

Q1 · Where did each market's sales change come from — which customers?

Company sales +120.7% (Jan–Apr): +189,151 from customers buying in both windows, +66,804 from 1998-window-only and -24,987 from 1997-window-only buyers; Canada's and Denmark's declines trace to one 1997-window-only account each.

Q2 · How much do sales depend on a few customers?

Top 1/5/10 customers hold 9.9%/34.1%/46.5% of 1997 sales; 33 customers make up 80.2%.

Q3 · Which order groups take longer to ship?

Median order-to-shipment time is 5–8 days in every country with 10+ orders (6–7 days by shipper); orders assigned to United Package and those for the USA and Germany make up most of the slow tail.

Q4 · What drives each category's sales change?

Most category growth goes with the quantity effect (+181,916 of +230,968); Dairy Products and Seafood grew broadly; Meat/Poultry's average price jump is mainly associated with product-mix change, with like-for-like price changes also contributing.

Q5 · How do quantity and sales differ by discount level?

Across 56 comparable products, the median of each product's ratio (median discounted quantity ÷ median undiscounted quantity) is 1.19. This summarises products; it does not mean every discounted line bought 19% more. Only Confections' interval excludes 1 — a lead for testing, not proof.

How the analysis was done

  • Data. Northwind Database.xlsx from the course spreadsheet (Google Sheets), read-only. Eight sheets; orders join to order lines by OrderID, to customers by CustomerID, to shippers by ShipVia, and lines join to products and categories. All joins were checked for lost or duplicated rows.
  • Sales. Discounted sales of each order line, UnitPrice × Quantity × (1 − Discount), at full precision and rounded only for display. Freight is excluded. There is no currency field and no cost field, so amounts carry no currency and are never called profit.
  • Grain. Orders are counted as unique OrderIDs and freight is taken at order level, so joining to order lines never inflates order counts.
  • Periods. Only 1997 is a complete year. Q1 and Q4 compare January–April of 1997 and 1998, which reduces seasonal differences; Q2, Q3 and Q5 use the full year 1997, by order date.
  • Checks. Every question reconciles its totals with independently computed totals. Q1–Q3 also trace one example back to the raw rows; Q4 and Q5 do not:
    • Q1: 44 reconciliation checks, all passed (q1_validation.csv). Spot-check: Switzerland traced to its raw orders and order lines (lines).
    • Q2: 23 reconciliation checks, all passed (q2_validation.csv). Spot-check: customer RATTC traced to its raw order lines (lines).
    • Q3: 17 reconciliation checks, all passed (q3_validation.csv). Spot-check: order 10777's shipping days recomputed from the Excel date serials.
    • Q4: 34 reconciliation checks, all passed (q4_validation.csv). No single-record spot-check. Totals and join counts are reconciled, and every comparable order line in the 1997 window is compared with its product's 1998 price.
    • Q5: 16 reconciliation checks, all passed (q5_validation.csv). No single-record spot-check. Totals are reconciled and every 1997 line is checked to fall in exactly one discount band.
